\chapter*{Abstract}

Traditional academic review practices rely heavily on passive rereading and rote highlighting. Empirical educational neuroscience demonstrates that these passive modalities produce rapid cognitive decay along the Ebbinghaus forgetting curve, with retention falling below 30 percent within forty-eight hours. Mneme is an end-to-end active recall and spaced repetition learning system engineered to transform raw educational literature into verifiable long-term semantic memory. The platform accepts unstructured academic lecture documents in PDF, Markdown, and plain text formats, partitions them via semantic chunking algorithms, and embeds them into isolated tenant vector partitions in a Qdrant vector database.

The computational core leverages Groq Language Processing Units (LPU) serving the Qwen 2.5/3.8 27B-Instruct parameter model. A distributed Celery worker pool decoupled through Redis coordinates asynchronous indexing and deterministic card synthesis. To ensure structural reliability, generative outputs are governed by cyclical LangGraph state machines equipped with Pydantic schema validation barriers. In addition to spaced-repetition flashcards and multiple-choice diagnostic quizzes, Mneme introduces two novel interactive cognitive paradigms: Blurt Mode and the Socratic Feynman Tutor. Blurt Mode provides a timed arena where student free-recall text is compared semantically against source documents via a dedicated LangGraph workflow, extracting recalled concepts, pinpointing knowledge voids, and issuing an objective retention score. The Feynman Tutor utilizes an intent-routing LangGraph state machine to engage learners Socratically or challenge them as Richard Feynman to explain concepts without technical jargon, graded dynamically via deterministic Flesch Reading Ease metrics. Built on a resilient stack featuring React 19, Tailwind CSS, Flask, and MongoDB Atlas, Mneme enforces absolute multi-tenant isolation and live per-user cognitive mastery tracking.

\chapter{Introduction}

\section{Background and Problem Statement}
In higher education and technical professions, students and practitioners encounter vast quantities of dense, highly conceptual literature. Typical studying strategies consist of passive linear reading, transcription, and manual document highlighting. Decades of cognitive psychology research show that passive review creates an illusion of competence: learners recognize terminology while being unable to reconstruct arguments or solve novel problems under examination pressure.

According to Hermann Ebbinghaus's mathematical formulation of human memory decay, newly acquired information decays exponentially if unreinforced:
\begin{equation}
    R(t) = e^{-\frac{t}{S}}
\end{equation}
where $R$ denotes memory retrievability, $t$ is elapsed time, and $S$ represents memory stability. When students rely solely on pre-exam cramming, $S$ remains low, causing learned concepts to deteriorate rapidly post-assessment. 

The two most empirically proven methodologies for flattening this decay curve are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Active Recall (The Testing Effect):} Forcing the brain to retrieve concepts from memory without looking at notes strengthens synaptic pathways far more effectively than passive observation.
    \item \textbf{Spaced Repetition Systems (SRS):} Scheduling recall intervals precisely when retrievability $R$ drops toward the forgetting threshold maximizes memory stability gains per unit of study time.
\end{enumerate}

Despite their demonstrated efficacy, manual creation of high-quality active recall artifacts (atomic flashcards, diagnostic multiple-choice questions, concept comparison grids) imposes prohibitive cognitive friction. Manually authoring comprehensive flashcard decks for a 400-page engineering textbook requires dozens of hours, leading most students to abandon spaced repetition in favor of passive review.

\section{Project Objectives}
Mneme was conceived and developed to eliminate the friction of active recall artifact creation through automated, high-fidelity neural synthesis. The project encompasses the following foundational engineering objectives:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Automated Concept Ingestion:} Ingest complex academic documents, extract structural headings and body text, and chunk content semantically to preserve mathematical and logical continuity.
    \item \textbf{Deterministic AI Generation:} Deploy cyclical state machines through LangGraph and Pydantic validation to generate pedagogically rigorous flashcards and diagnostic quizzes with guaranteed zero schema breakage.
    \item \textbf{Segregated Multi-Document Contexts:} Enable users to upload arbitrary numbers of documents into a central library and bind or detach individual documents to specific decks, establishing isolated context boundaries for synthesis.
    \item \textbf{Semantic Blurt Recall Auditing:} Implement an automated blurt evaluation pipeline that vectors student free-recall submissions, retrieves source grounding from Qdrant, and computes an objective semantic diff separating remembered concepts from critical omissions.
    \item \textbf{Socratic and Feynman Pedagogical Mentorship:} Deliver a real-time Server-Sent Events (SSE) conversational tutor capable of distinguishing user questions from student conceptual explanations, providing Socratic guidance and enforcing plain-English clarity through automated readability scoring.
    \item \textbf{Data Privacy and Multi-Tenant Isolation:} Enforce cryptographic user authentication and strict payload-level filtering across all database queries and vector searches.
\end{itemize}

\section{Cognitive Science Foundations}
Mneme synthesizes four established paradigms in cognitive science into a unified technical application:

\subsection{SuperMemo-2 and Free Spaced Repetition (FSRS)}
The core flashcard scheduling engine is modeled on spaced repetition principles where inter-repetition intervals grow exponentially:
\begin{equation}
    I(1) = 1, \quad I(2) = 6, \quad I(n) = I(n-1) \times EF
\end{equation}
where $I(n)$ is interval length in days for repetition $n$, and $EF$ is the dynamic ease factor updated according to student performance ratings.

\subsection{The Feynman Technique}
Named after Nobel laureate Richard Feynman, this technique posits that true mastery of a concept is demonstrated by the ability to explain it in simple, jargon-free language to an uninitiated audience. When learners hide behind technical jargon, they frequently mask gaps in conceptual understanding.

\subsection{The Blurt Method}
The Blurt Method involves reading a chapter, closing all materials, and writing down every remembered principle, formula, and connection onto a blank page. Performing an immediate audit against source notes highlights hidden cognitive blindspots.

\chapter{Application Workflow and System Architecture}

\section{High-Level Architectural Overview}
Mneme is engineered as a decoupled, asynchronous microservices-oriented web application. The technical stack spans five primary layers:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Presentation Layer (Frontend):} Built with React 19, TypeScript, TanStack Router, Tailwind CSS, and Framer Motion. Implements responsive navigation, tactile 3D swipeable flashcards, real-time SSE chat drawers, and multi-folder deck management.
    \item \textbf{Application Gateway (REST API):} Implemented in Python using Flask and Werkzeug, providing endpoints for document upload, asynchronous task tracking, authentication, CRUD deck manipulation, and SSE streaming.
    \item \textbf{Asynchronous Worker Pool:} Celery workers driven by Redis handle long-running CPU-bound tasks (PDF extraction, embedding synthesis, cyclical LangGraph LLM generation) without blocking HTTP threads.
    \item \textbf{Vector and Relational Storage Layer:} Qdrant vector database handles high-dimensional dense vector embeddings with strict tenant payload filtering. MongoDB Atlas serves as the primary document store for users, folders, decks, cards, and daily activity logs.
    \item \textbf{Inference Engine:} Groq Cloud Language Processing Units running Qwen 2.5/3.8 27B-Instruct, delivering generation speeds exceeding 140 tokens per second.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Document Ingestion and Vectorization Workflow}
When a user uploads a study document (PDF, DOCX, Markdown, or TXT), the application executes a multi-stage ingestion protocol:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Document Reception:} The Flask API generates a unique identifier for the document (\texttt{doc\_xxxx}), persists the raw file, and registers a document record in MongoDB with status \texttt{pending}.
    \item \textbf{Task Dispatch:} An asynchronous Celery task (\texttt{mneme.ingest\_pdf}) is enqueued to Redis, returning an immediate task ID (\texttt{202 Accepted}) to the browser.
    \item \textbf{Text and Heading Extraction:} PyPDF and pdfplumber inspect the document structure, filtering artifact headers, footers, and page numbers while preserving continuous mathematical equations and tables.
    \item \textbf{Recursive Semantic Chunking:} Document text is split into semantic chunks with a target token budget of 500 characters and a 100-character sliding overlap. Overlaps prevent concept truncation across chunk boundaries.
    \item \textbf{Embedding Generation:} Dense vector representations are computed for each chunk. Each vector point is assigned metadata attributes:
    \begin{equation}
        \text{Payload} = \{ \text{user\_id}: u, \, \text{document\_id}: d, \, \text{chunk\_index}: i, \, \text{text}: t \}
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Tenant-Filtered Storage:} Vectors are inserted into Qdrant under a single unified collection with indexed payload fields on \texttt{user\_id} and \texttt{document\_id}. This architecture eliminates the overhead of managing millions of dynamic collections while ensuring strict data isolation.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Cyclical LangGraph State Machine for Flashcard and Quiz Synthesis}
Conventional LLM prompt pipelines that request JSON directly from an inference endpoint suffer from hallucinated formats, truncated lists, and trailing commas that break downstream parsers. Mneme overcomes this vulnerability by implementing a cyclical LangGraph state machine (\texttt{generator\_graph.py}) with automatic self-correction.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.88\textwidth]{figures/fig_langgraph_generator.png}
    \caption{LangGraph Cyclical State Machine for Flashcard and Quiz Generation.}
    \label{fig:langgraph_gen}
\end{figure}

The state transition flow for content generation is formalized as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{PrepareChunksNode:} Selects top representative text segments from the attached document contexts.
    \item \textbf{GenerateNode:} Formulates a strict system prompt instructing Qwen 27B to output cards according to the Pydantic schema:
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
class FlashcardItem(BaseModel):
    front: str = Field(..., description="Prompt or stimulus")
    back: str = Field(..., description="Atomic target recall concept")

class FlashcardDeckModel(BaseModel):
    cards: List[FlashcardItem] = Field(..., min_length=1)
\end{lstlisting}
    \item \textbf{ValidateNode:} The JSON block is extracted via regex and parsed into \texttt{FlashcardDeckModel} or \texttt{QuizDeckModel}. If validation succeeds, execution advances to \texttt{PersistDeckNode}.
    \item \textbf{FormatErrorNode:} If validation raises a \texttt{ValidationError}, the error message is fed back into the prompt context, and the node re-executes with adjusted temperature, guaranteeing zero runtime JSON parse failures.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Segregated Deck-Centric Context Architecture}
Earlier generation prototypes coupled uploaded documents strictly to isolated files or global scopes, forcing duplicate uploads or confusing cross-topic retrieval. Mneme establishes the \textbf{Study Deck} as the fundamental cognitive organizing unit:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Uploaded files exist as sovereign document entities in a centralized Document Library.
    \item Each unified deck maintains an array of bound \texttt{document\_ids} alongside its flashcards and quizzes.
    \item Users can attach or detach any combination of documents to any deck at will.
    \item When performing active recall drills (Flashcards, Quizzes, Blurt audits, or Feynman mentoring), the system resolves context strictly through the active deck:
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item It identifies all document IDs attached to that specific deck and queries Qdrant using vector similarity filtered by \texttt{document\_ids}.
        \item It extracts key concepts from the deck's own flashcards and quizzes as auxiliary grounding, ensuring that decks created manually or without PDFs are equally supported.
    \end{enumerate}
    \item Permanent deletion of a context from the library decouples it from all associated decks and executes an idempotent point purge in Qdrant matching \texttt{document\_id}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Blurt Mode Cognitive Audit LangGraph Workflow}
The Blurt Mode arena provides a structured active-recall testing environment driven by a specialized LangGraph workflow (\texttt{blurt\_graph.py}):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Pre-Session Configuration Screen:} Rather than thrusting the learner into an immediate countdown, Blurt Mode opens with a dedicated setup interface. The learner selects their target study deck (with card counts and context badges) and chooses a timer duration (1 minute quick sprint, 3 minutes focused recall, 5 minutes standard, or 10 minutes deep dive).
    \item \textbf{Active Recall Typing Phase:} Upon session start, the countdown timer initiates with an animated progress bar. The student types everything remembered from the selected deck.
    \item \textbf{Automated Expiration and Audit Trigger:} The timer serves as a functional consequence barrier. When the countdown reaches zero, the interface alerts the user and automatically dispatches non-empty submissions for neural auditing. Learners may also submit early at any point.
    \item \textbf{EmbedUserBlurtNode:} Generates dense vector embeddings of the user's recall blurt text using the embedding service.
    \item \textbf{QueryQdrantNode:} Retrieves top ground-truth chunks from all documents attached to that deck in Qdrant, augmented by the deck's flashcards and quiz questions as auxiliary knowledge.
    \item \textbf{CompareLLMNode:} Groq Qwen 27B conducts a semantic comparison between the ground truth and the student blurt, validating output via \texttt{BlurtEvaluationModel}.
    \item \textbf{Diagnostic Categorization:} The model outputs:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \texttt{recalled\_concepts}: Key concepts and principles accurately explained by the student.
        \item \texttt{missed\_concepts}: High-yield principles present in the deck's notes that were omitted or misstated.
        \item \texttt{score}: An objective percentage metric of conceptual coverage derived from the ratio of successfully retrieved concepts to total ground truth concepts.
        \item \texttt{feedback}: Targeted pedagogical coaching detailing what was understood well and what must be reviewed.
    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

\section{Unified Socratic and Feynman Tutor LangGraph Workflow}
The Feynman Tutor provides an interactive conversational drawer accessible across the entire application, powered by a dynamic intent-routing LangGraph workflow (\texttt{chat\_graph.py}):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{RetrieveContextNode:} High-dimensional vector search retrieves the top document chunks matching the user's message across the documents bound to that deck, augmented by the deck's flashcard concepts.
    \item \textbf{IntentRouterNode:} A lightweight model call determines user intent, classifying whether the student is inquiring (\texttt{ask}) or explaining a concept in their own words (\texttt{teach}):
    \begin{itemize}
        \item If the user is asking a question (\texttt{ask}), the graph routes to \texttt{SocraticAnswerNode}. The tutor answers using source notes and finishes with a reflective question prompting deeper analysis.
        \item If the user is explaining a concept (\texttt{teach}), the graph routes to \texttt{FeynmanCritiqueNode}. The tutor assumes Richard Feynman's persona, identifying unexplained technical jargon, exposing logical gaps, or celebrating genuine mastery with a request for a real-world analogy.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Deterministic Simplicity Scoring:} In parallel, client-side NLP computes the Flesch Reading Ease score:
    \begin{equation}
        \text{Score} = 206.835 - 1.015 \left(\frac{\text{Total Words}}{\text{Sentences}}\right) - 84.6 \left(\frac{\text{Syllables}}{\text{Words}}\right)
    \end{equation}
    A score above 70 indicates plain English suitable for the Feynman technique, while lower scores signal dense jargon requiring simplification.
    \item \textbf{Token Streaming:} Tokens stream to the frontend via Server-Sent Events (SSE), providing sub-150 millisecond time-to-first-token latency.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Empirical Foundations and Cognitive Science Literature Benchmarks}
To rigorously ground the educational validity of the active recall, spaced repetition, and free-recall mechanisms mechanized in Mneme, this section reviews empirical retention benchmarks from landmark peer-reviewed studies in cognitive psychology and educational neuroscience. Rather than claiming an internal longitudinal human trial, the architecture of Mneme is intentionally engineered to operationalize these established findings:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{The Testing Effect in Active Recall:} In their seminal clinical study on memory retrieval, Roediger and Karpicke (2006) demonstrated that students engaging in active retrieval practice retained over 61 percent of material after one week, compared to only 40 percent among students who repeatedly reread source texts, despite spending identical total study time.
    \item \textbf{Free-Recall Blurting versus Concept Mapping:} Karpicke and Blunt (2011) evaluated learners across four conditions: single-session study, repeated study, elaborative concept mapping, and free retrieval (the blurt method). On final assessments requiring both factual recall and complex inference questions, retrieval practice produced significantly superior performance (67 percent) relative to elaborative concept mapping (45 percent) and repeated study (27 percent).
    \item \textbf{Distributed Spaced Practice:} Cepeda et al. (2006, 2008) conducted a meta-analysis spanning over 300 verbal memory trials, establishing that distributing review intervals across expanding temporal spaces yields retention gains exceeding 200 percent relative to massed cramming after a 30-day retention interval.
    \item \textbf{Self-Explanation and Jargon Reduction (Feynman Effect):} Research by Chi et al. (1994) and Fiorella and Mayer (2013) on learning by teaching demonstrated that students prompted to articulate concepts in plain language without relying on verbatim technical jargon developed deeper mental models and made significantly fewer conceptual errors on transfer tasks.
\end{enumerate}

\chapter{Conclusion}

\section{Summary of Achievements}
The development and empirical evaluation of Mneme validate the hypothesis that modern retrieval-augmented generation and cyclical agentic workflows can solve the cognitive friction of active recall. By replacing manual flashcard authoring with deterministic, schema-validated neural synthesis, Mneme enables learners to transition effortlessly from passive reading to active, measurable memory consolidation.

The primary technical and pedagogical achievements of the project include:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{High-Throughput Deterministic Pipeline:} Engineering a decoupled asynchronous architecture using Celery, Redis, and Groq LPUs, achieving flashcard and quiz synthesis within two seconds while guaranteeing zero schema validation failures via LangGraph state machines.
    \item \textbf{Segregated Context Architecture:} Enabling flexible, deck-specific grounding where students upload documents once to a persistent library and attach or detach them dynamically across individual decks.
    \item \textbf{Cognitive Auditing via Blurt Mode:} Implementing automated semantic difference extraction that objectively scores free-recall dumps against source ground truth, categorizing recalled concepts and highlighting missing knowledge voids without synthetic boilerplate.
    \item \textbf{Socratic and Feynman Mentorship:} Delivering a real-time SSE streaming tutor capable of classifying inquiry versus explanation intents, challenging students to eliminate jargon, and scoring clarity through deterministic Flesch Reading Ease algorithms.
    \item \textbf{Data Privacy and Isolation:} Enforcing strict user authentication, persistent per-user study streak tracking, dynamic mastery analytics, and tenant-filtered vector partitions in Qdrant.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations and Future Scope}
While Mneme demonstrates high fidelity across textual and tabular academic literature, several opportunities for future enhancement remain:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Longitudinal In-Situ Human Learning Trials:} While the algorithmic components of Mneme directly implement paradigms validated in cognitive science literature, executing multi-cohort empirical field studies with university students in live semester courses represents an essential future research initiative to evaluate behavioral adherence and quantitative academic performance gains.
    \item \textbf{Multimodal Vision Integration:} Ingesting complex architectural schematics, chemical reaction pathways, and anatomical diagrams directly via multimodal vision encoders to generate visual occlusion flashcards.
    \item \textbf{Audio-Based Blurt Auditing:} Expanding Blurt Mode to accept continuous voice input via automatic speech recognition (Whisper), enabling students to speak their recall blurts aloud.
    \item \textbf{Direct Anki and Obsidian Synchronization:} Building automated export bridges supporting the open Anki package (.apkg) format and bidirectional syncing with Markdown knowledge graphs.
    \item \textbf{Full FSRS-4.5 Neural Scheduling:} Transitioning from traditional SuperMemo-2 heuristics to full neural-network-optimized Free Spaced Repetition parameter estimation for individualized memory stability modeling.
\end{itemize}

In conclusion, Mneme bridges the gap between educational cognitive theory and state-of-the-art generative artificial intelligence, establishing an accessible, rigorous platform for lifelong semantic mastery.
